# sdr-outbound-writing-rules

Use this skill to govern the structure, length, tone, and formatting of every outgoing SDR email.

## The Iron Laws of SDR Copy

1. **Word Count Limit:** Maximum 100 words. Optimal is 50-75 words.
2. **Mobile Optimization:** No paragraph may be longer than 3 lines on a mobile screen.
3. **The "I/We" Ratio:** The words "You" and "Your" must vastly outnumber "I", "Me", "We", and "Our".
4. **Readability:** Aim for a 6th-grade reading level. Remove all jargon, adverbs, and complex adjectives.

## Call to Action (CTA) Types

Never use "15 minutes?" on a cold email. It asks for too much commitment before value is proven. Use these instead:

### 1. The Interest CTA (Low Friction)
- "Worth sending over a quick 2-min video?"
- "Open to seeing the framework on how we did this?"

### 2. The Feedback CTA (Validation)
- "Am I way off base here, or is this a priority for Q3?"
- "Is this something your team is currently handling internally?"

### 3. The Deferral CTA (Routing)
- "Are you the best person to speak with about this, or is there someone else on the team handling X?"

## Structural Anti-Patterns (Never Do This)

- **The Bait and Switch:** Faking a reply thread (e.g., using "Re: " in a cold subject line).
- **The Fake Apology:** "I know you're busy, I'll be brief." (Don't say you'll be brief. Just be brief).
- **The Book Report:** "We are a B2B SaaS platform that utilizes machine learning to..." (Nobody cares what you are, they care what you fix).
- **The Aggressive Breakup:** "Since you clearly don't care about revenue..." (Burns the bridge forever).

## Output Contract
When reviewing or writing SDR emails, provide:
- A strict word count check.
- Identification of any "I/We" vs "You/Your" imbalance.
- A rewritten version enforcing the Iron Laws, stripping jargon, and utilizing a low-friction CTA.
